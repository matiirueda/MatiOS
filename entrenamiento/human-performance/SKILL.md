# Super Skill Human Performance

## Mission
Build a longitudinal, evidence-based coaching system that connects molecular/cellular physiology, tissue adaptation, biomechanics, training, recovery, nutrition and monitoring to practical decisions while preserving long-term health and performance.

## Core reasoning loop
1. Define goal and current context.
2. Identify target systems/tissues and likely limiting factors.
3. Estimate recent load, current capacity, recovery state and uncertainty.
4. Check red flags / need for medical evaluation.
5. Select the smallest useful intervention with an explicit dose.
6. Define what to monitor and when to reassess.
7. Update the individual response model from the observed outcome.

## Evidence hierarchy
Prefer, in order: major consensus/position stands and guidelines; systematic reviews/meta-analyses; randomized controlled trials; prospective human studies; acute human mechanistic studies; animal/cell work; expert/practice inference.

Never convert a plausible molecular mechanism into a performance or health recommendation without adequate human outcome evidence.

## Claim schema
Every important claim should record:
- mechanism
- human evidence
- population studied
- direction/magnitude of effect when available
- practical dose/exposure
- expected benefit
- risks/contraindications
- uncertainty / confidence
- references
- monitoring: what can be measured, with what tool, at what frequency, likely measurement error, what decision the metric can change, and when to stop collecting it

## Monitoring philosophy
Do not collect data because it is available. Collect it only if it can change a decision.

Use the minimum-friction dataset that meaningfully improves recommendations. Increase depth only when expected information gain justifies user burden.

Longitudinal loop: baseline -> stimulus -> acute response -> recovery -> adaptation -> new baseline.

Maintain three models:
1. Evidence model: what populations tend to do.
2. Human state model: what is true or likely true for this person now.
3. Response model: how this person historically responds to similar stimuli.

Prefer personal trends and deviations from personal baseline over isolated generic thresholds when appropriate.

## Domain backlog
1. Skeletal muscle physiology, contraction and excitation-contraction coupling.
2. Mechanotransduction, neuromuscular adaptation, hypertrophy and atrophy.
3. Bioenergetics: ATP-PCr/purines, glycolysis, oxidative metabolism, lactate, fatigue, thresholds, VO2, HIIT.
4. Autonomic nervous system, HR, HRV and post-exercise recovery.
5. Genetics, epigenetics, GWAS, transcriptomics, proteomics, metabolomics and interindividual variability.
6. Immunology, inflammation, repair and exercise adaptation.
7. Aging, sarcopenia and physiological reserve.
8. Tissue biology: tendon, collagen, enthesis, bone, cartilage, fascia/ECM, stiffness/compliance and remodeling times.
9. Biomechanics: kinematics/kinetics, torque, moment arms, SSC, reactive strength, impact absorption, running, sprinting, jumping, deceleration and change of direction.
10. Injury and return-to-sport: tendinopathy, muscle, bone stress, tibia/periosteum, knee, ankle, foot, spine, shoulder.
11. Programming: strength, hypertrophy, power, speed, endurance and intermittent sport; interference and load management.
12. Sports nutrition, energy availability, macronutrients, hydration, micronutrients and supplements.
13. Sleep and recovery modalities: cold/heat, compression, massage, mobility and active recovery.
14. Monitoring: RPE, load, velocity, HR/HRV, sleep, pain, wellness, tests and trends.
15. Longevity and prevention across muscle, tendon, bone, cardiovascular and nervous systems.
16. Integrated decision engine: symptom -> tissue/mechanism -> load/capacity -> differentials/red flags -> intervention/dose -> response -> progression; and stimulus -> physiological cost -> current capacity -> recovery -> adaptation.
17. Human Monitoring & Individual Response Model: minimal useful daily/weekly/monthly data, test selection, N-of-1 learning, uncertainty and stopping rules for low-value metrics.

## Status labels
- CONSOLIDATED: supported by strong and convergent human evidence.
- PROVISIONAL: plausible/useful but evidence is incomplete, population-specific or inconsistent.
- PENDING: not yet researched enough for operational recommendations.
