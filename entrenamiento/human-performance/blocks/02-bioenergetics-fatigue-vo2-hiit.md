# Block 02 — Bioenergetics, fatigue, thresholds, VO2 and HIIT

Status: **MOSTLY CONSOLIDATED** for the core physiology and broad training implications; **PROVISIONAL** where protocol-specific claims, individual prediction or field surrogates exceed current evidence.

Last research pass: 2026-09-14.

## Scope
ATP turnover; phosphocreatine (PCr); adenylate kinase / AMP deamination / purine loss; glycolysis; oxidative phosphorylation; lactate; H+ / pH; inorganic phosphate (Pi); fatigue; exercise-intensity domains; lactate/ventilatory thresholds; critical power (CP); maximal lactate steady state (MLSS); VO2max; VO2 kinetics; HIIT/SIT/RST; monitoring and decision rules.

## Operational model for Human Performance

Do not model energy systems as three isolated switches. During exercise they overlap continuously, and their relative contribution changes with intensity, duration, pacing, substrate availability, muscle recruited, training status and recovery interval.

The coach should reason in this order:

`task demand -> ATP turnover rate -> dominant energetic constraints -> metabolite/ion consequences -> cardiorespiratory response -> perceived effort -> recoverability -> adaptation target`

Never infer a single causal mechanism of fatigue from a symptom such as “burning”, high lactate, high RPE or performance loss alone.

## Claim 1 — ATP concentration is defended; PCr is a rapid temporal buffer for ATP demand

**Status:** CONSOLIDATED

**Mechanism**
- ATP is the immediate energy currency for cross-bridge cycling, ion pumps and cellular work.
- During abrupt increases in demand, creatine kinase rapidly transfers phosphate from PCr to ADP, buffering the ATP/ADP ratio while oxidative phosphorylation and glycolysis increase.
- During recovery, PCr is resynthesized mainly using ATP produced by oxidative metabolism; therefore PCr recovery kinetics can index local oxidative capacity under controlled conditions.

**Human evidence**
- 31P-MRS studies show rapid PCr depletion during brief maximal exercise while ATP is relatively defended, followed by recovery related to oxidative capacity.
- In one human gastrocnemius experiment, ~61% PCr depletion occurred after 9 s of maximal-rate work and ~92% after 30 s; PCr recovery kinetics tracked muscle oxidative capacity, although marked acidosis can distort simple monoexponential interpretation.

**Population**
Mostly healthy young adults in small mechanistic laboratory studies.

**Direction / magnitude**
PCr contribution is greatest when ATP demand rises faster than oxidative ATP production can respond. Exact percentages are task-dependent and should not be generalized from one protocol.

**Dose / application**
- Very short explosive work strongly stresses PCr turnover.
- Longer recovery between maximal bouts allows more PCr restoration and therefore better preservation of peak power.
- The recovery interval required for a specific sport or session should be chosen from the goal: preserve peak power vs intentionally accumulate fatigue.

**Risks / contraindications**
None specific to the mechanism; exercise contraindications come from the underlying task and health context.

**Confidence:** HIGH for mechanism; MODERATE for translating a laboratory PCr time constant directly into field programming.

**References**
- Walter G et al. Am J Physiol. 1997. PMID 9124295. DOI 10.1152/ajpcell.1997.272.2.C525.
- Forbes SC et al. J Appl Physiol. 2009. PMID 18945946.
- Arnold DL et al. Magn Reson Med. 1984. PMID 6571561.

**Monitoring**
Field coaching should usually monitor repeated-bout power/speed decay and recovery, not PCr directly. 31P-MRS is a research/clinical-layer measurement, not a routine user task.

## Claim 2 — “Anaerobic” and “aerobic” systems overlap from exercise onset

**Status:** CONSOLIDATED

**Mechanism**
Oxidative phosphorylation begins contributing immediately, while PCr breakdown and glycolysis cover the mismatch between ATP demand and oxidative ATP supply. Relative contributions change continuously rather than switching at a fixed time.

**Human evidence**
A 2026 systematic review of 102 studies / 311 data points on maximal exercise found an estimated equal anaerobic:aerobic energetic contribution near 78.6 s (95% CI about ±1.1 s) on average, with shorter efforts tending to be more anaerobically weighted and longer efforts increasingly aerobic. Measurement method and pacing influenced estimates.

**Population**
Adults; evidence base predominantly male and exercise-mode dependent.

**Application**
- Do not label 30-s work as “pure anaerobic” or 2-min work as “pure aerobic”.
- Repeated sprint sports require both rapid anaerobic ATP provision and strong oxidative recovery between efforts.

**Confidence:** HIGH for overlap; MODERATE for any exact crossover time in an individual.

**Reference**
- Anaerobic and Aerobic Energy System Contribution During Maximal Exercise: A Systematic Review. 2026. PMID 41965479.

## Claim 3 — Lactate is not simply a waste product or the direct cause of fatigue

**Status:** CONSOLIDATED

**Mechanism**
- Lactate is produced during glycolysis under fully aerobic as well as high-intensity conditions.
- It is transported among cells and organs via monocarboxylate transporters and can be oxidized as fuel or used as a carbon carrier for gluconeogenesis and glycogen-related metabolism.
- Rising blood lactate reflects the balance of production, transport and clearance; it is not a direct meter of “lack of oxygen”.

**Human evidence**
- Human tracer and physiological work demonstrates lactate production and shuttling under aerobic conditions.
- A 2024 human tracer study demonstrated postprandial lactate shuttling after glucose ingestion, reinforcing that lactate formation is compatible with fully aerobic metabolism.
- A 2025 review concluded that lactate concentration itself has little direct detrimental effect on muscle performance at physiological/high exercise concentrations.

**Population**
Healthy humans, exercise and metabolic tracer studies.

**Application**
- Never tell the user that lactate is “toxic waste” that must be flushed.
- Do not infer tissue hypoxia from elevated exercise lactate in a healthy exercising person.
- Blood lactate can still be useful operationally for intensity-domain testing when collected with a standardized protocol.

**Confidence:** HIGH.

**References**
- Brooks GA. Redox Biology. 2020. PMID 32444344.
- Leija RG et al. Nat Metab. 2024. PMID 38388706.
- Lactic acidosis: implications for human exercise performance. 2025. PMID 40088272.

## Claim 4 — Acidosis can contribute to intense-exercise fatigue, but it is not the sole cause

**Status:** CONSOLIDATED with mechanistic nuance

**Mechanism**
Severe intracellular acidosis and associated protonated phosphate species can impair cross-bridge function, Ca2+ sensitivity and myosin ATPase activity. However, Pi accumulation, K+ disturbances, impaired Ca2+ release, glycogen status, ROS, neural drive and afferent feedback also contribute depending on task.

**Human evidence / magnitude**
A 2025 review reports that severe experimentally relevant acidosis around intracellular pH ~6.5–6.2 can reduce maximal force by roughly 12%, shortening velocity by ~5% and peak power by ~22% in non-fatigued preparations/individuals, while milder acidosis has much smaller effects. The relationship between pH and performance varies across exercise tasks.

**Application**
- “The burn” is not a complete diagnosis of fatigue.
- Buffering strategies should only be recommended when human performance evidence supports them for the task; mechanistic plausibility alone is insufficient.

**Confidence:** HIGH that acidosis can contribute; HIGH that it is not the sole mechanism.

**Reference**
- Lactic acidosis: implications for human exercise performance. 2025. PMID 40088272.

## Claim 5 — Inorganic phosphate (Pi) is a major peripheral-fatigue candidate during intense contractions

**Status:** CONSOLIDATED for contribution; PROVISIONAL for exact individual attribution

**Mechanism**
PCr breakdown increases intracellular Pi. Elevated Pi can impair myofibrillar cross-bridge performance and reduce SR Ca2+ release, decreasing force-generating capacity.

**Human evidence**
31P-MRS and mechanistic work strongly associate phosphate disturbances with fatigue. A recent human model combining in-vivo 31P-MRS and activation data supports metabolite-mediated inhibition of cross-bridge cycling as one contributor to task failure.

**Application**
Human Performance should represent fatigue as a multi-factor state, with Pi as a plausible contributor when intense repeated contractions cause rapid PCr breakdown; it should not tell a user “your Pi is high” without direct measurement.

**Confidence:** HIGH mechanistically; LOW for field attribution in an individual.

**References**
- Allen DG, Trajanovska S. Front Physiol. 2012. PMID 23248600.
- Human skeletal muscle cross-bridge model... 2025. PMID 40449991.

## Claim 6 — AMP deamination / purine loss is a marker of severe energetic stress, not a routine performance target

**Status:** CONSOLIDATED mechanism; PROVISIONAL practical use

**Mechanism**
When ATP resynthesis cannot fully match demand, ADP and AMP rise transiently. AMP can be deaminated to IMP + ammonia, helping preserve adenylate energy charge but potentially leading to downstream purine loss (inosine/hypoxanthine) during recovery.

**Human evidence**
Intense exercise studies with muscle biopsy show AMP deamination, IMP and ammonia accumulation and measurable loss of purine metabolites after exhaustive work. Low glycogen and high metabolic stress can increase adenine nucleotide degradation.

**Application**
- Do not use blood ammonia or purines as routine monitoring variables.
- For coaching, their main value is conceptual: severe energetic stress has a recovery cost that is not captured by lactate alone.

**Confidence:** HIGH mechanism; LOW utility for routine monitoring.

**References**
- Hellsten Y et al. J Physiol. 1999. PMID 10545153.
- Sahlin K, Broberg S. Int J Sports Med. 1990. PMID 2361781.
- Adenine nucleotide degradation in human skeletal muscle during prolonged exercise. PMID 2759935.

## Claim 7 — Glycolysis and oxidative phosphorylation are coupled by task intensity, substrate availability and mitochondrial/cardiorespiratory capacity

**Status:** CONSOLIDATED broad model

**Mechanism**
Glycolytic flux rises rapidly with ATP demand and provides pyruvate/lactate; mitochondrial oxidation supplies increasingly large absolute and relative ATP flux as oxygen uptake rises. Carbohydrate availability becomes especially important as exercise intensity rises because it supports high rates of ATP turnover.

**Application**
- Do not prescribe low-carbohydrate availability as a default for high-intensity performance.
- Fueling decisions must be linked to session objective, duration, total energy availability and later-day/next-day training.
- Detailed nutrition dosing belongs in the nutrition block; this block only establishes the energetic rationale.

**Confidence:** HIGH broad physiology; dosing deferred to nutrition block.

## Claim 8 — Exercise intensity should be modeled by physiological domains rather than universal %HRmax/%VO2max alone

**Status:** MOSTLY CONSOLIDATED

**Mechanism / concept**
Physiological responses differ qualitatively across moderate, heavy and severe intensity domains. The transition near the maximal metabolic steady state separates work where metabolic variables can stabilize from work that produces progressive disturbance until task failure.

**Human evidence**
CP and MLSS are widely used to estimate the heavy–severe boundary. A 2024 methodological review argues that strict determination can bring the two estimates close, while a 2025 systematic review/meta-analysis still found CP averaging ~12 W above MLSS with wide individual limits of agreement (~-19 to +44 W). Thus they are related but not perfectly interchangeable at individual level.

**Application**
- Prefer individualized thresholds when precision matters.
- Generic heart-rate zones are acceptable as low-friction approximations but should carry more uncertainty.
- Do not equate “Zone 2” with a single universal %HRmax. A 2025 expert viewpoint preferred intensities immediately below the first lactate/ventilatory threshold.

**Confidence:** HIGH for domain concept; MODERATE for interchangeability among threshold methods.

**References**
- Caen K et al. Sports Med. 2024. PMID 39196486. DOI 10.1007/s40279-024-02075-4.
- Micheli L et al. PeerJ. 2025. PMID 40124604.
- What Is “Zone 2 Training”? Experts' Viewpoint... 2025. PMID 40010355.

## Claim 9 — VO2max is an integrated systems outcome, not a direct measure of mitochondrial quality alone

**Status:** CONSOLIDATED

**Mechanism**
VO2 = cardiac output × arteriovenous O2 difference (Fick principle). VO2max therefore integrates pulmonary gas exchange, blood O2 carrying capacity, maximal cardiac output, perfusion distribution and peripheral extraction. In healthy exercising humans, oxygen delivery is often a major limitation, while muscle oxidative adaptations strongly influence submaximal performance and exercise economy.

**Application**
- A change in VO2max does not identify which subsystem changed.
- Endurance performance should not be predicted from VO2max alone; thresholds/fractional utilization, economy/efficiency and sport-specific power/speed also matter.

**Confidence:** HIGH.

**Reference**
- Bassett DR, Howley ET. Med Sci Sports Exerc. 2000. PMID 10647532.

## Claim 10 — HIIT reliably improves cardiorespiratory fitness, but there is no single universally optimal protocol

**Status:** CONSOLIDATED for efficacy; PROVISIONAL for “optimal” protocol claims

**Human evidence / magnitude**
- A 2024 umbrella review found HIIT/SIT consistently improved cardiorespiratory fitness versus inactive control and generally produced small-to-moderate additional gains versus MICT. Across included meta-analyses, weighted mean differences versus control were roughly 3.25–5.5 mL/kg/min and versus MICT ~0.52–3.76 mL/kg/min, depending heavily on population/protocol.
- A 2019 meta-analysis of 53 trials found longer intervals (>=2 min), higher accumulated high-intensity volume (>=15 min) and interventions lasting >=4 weeks tended to produce larger VO2max effects than lower-volume/shorter protocols.
- A 2025 athlete network meta-analysis found HIIT, SIT and repeated-sprint training all improved VO2max, without statistically significant differences among the three despite different probabilistic rankings.
- A 2026 network meta-analysis focused on time >=90% VO2max found substantial heterogeneity/inconsistency and no HIIT design clearly superior to even long intervals for that acute surrogate.

**Application**
Protocol selection should follow adaptation target, sport, training age, injury/load tolerance, available time and total weekly stress. Do not optimize solely for “time above 90% VO2max”; acute surrogate maximization is not equivalent to proven superior long-term adaptation.

**Practical starting archetypes (not universal prescriptions)**
- Aerobic-power emphasis: repeated 2–4 min work bouts near severe-domain intensity, with enough recovery to sustain high oxygen uptake and technique.
- Sprint-power / repeated-sprint emphasis: much shorter near-maximal/maximal bouts with recovery chosen according to whether the target is peak quality or fatigue tolerance.
- Low-volume HIIT can improve VO2max, especially in less trained populations, but “minimum effective” should not be confused with “maximally effective”.

**Risks / contraindications**
Higher mechanical and cardiovascular stress than easy continuous work. Progression, health status and musculoskeletal capacity matter. Running HIIT adds tissue load that cycling/rowing may reduce.

**Confidence:** HIGH efficacy; MODERATE protocol-level superiority.

**References**
- High-intensity interval training and cardiorespiratory fitness in adults: umbrella review. 2024. PMID 38760916.
- Wen D et al. Scand J Med Sci Sports. 2019. PMID 30733142.
- Comparison of different interval training methods on athletes' oxygen uptake. 2025. PMID 40605061.
- Comparison of HIIT protocol designs on accumulated time >=90% VO2max. 2026. PMID 42482078.

## Claim 11 — Individual “non-response” must be separated from measurement error

**Status:** CONSOLIDATED methodological rule

**Evidence**
A 2024 systematic review/meta-analysis examining individual VO2max responses concluded that much observed change-score variability can be explained by measurement error and found weak evidence for a stable, intervention-specific individual trainability variance.

Wearable VO2max estimates can be useful for population/trend screening but have wide individual limits of agreement compared with laboratory measurement; exercise-based algorithms outperform resting-only algorithms but individual error remains substantial.

**Application**
Before Human labels someone a low responder:
1. verify test protocol and device consistency;
2. compare change against test typical error / minimal detectable change when known;
3. repeat or triangulate with sport-specific performance;
4. verify adherence and dose;
5. only then update the individual response model.

**Confidence:** HIGH methodological principle.

**References**
- Standard Deviation of Individual Response for VO2max Following Exercise Interventions. 2024. PMID 39160296.
- INTERLIVE wearable VO2max systematic review/meta-analysis. 2022. PMID 35072942.

# Monitoring layer

## Minimum useful dataset
For most users, do not ask for lactate, ammonia, PCr or laboratory metabolic data.

Low-friction monitoring:
- session type and modality
- duration
- session RPE
- key interval work/rest structure
- power/pace/speed where available
- HR only when device quality/context make it useful
- next-day fatigue / soreness when load tolerance is relevant
- periodic standardized performance test chosen for goal

## Optional higher-resolution layer
- chest-strap HR and recovery kinetics
- laboratory/field threshold test using standardized protocol
- blood lactate when a trained operator and explicit decision use exist
- VO2max/CPET when its result will materially change training/clinical decisions
- power-duration testing / critical power for cyclists/runners with reliable data

## Measurement rules
- Same protocol > more frequent testing.
- Prefer repeated standardized conditions.
- Compare against personal baseline and test error.
- Never interpret a wearable VO2max estimate as an exact laboratory VO2max.
- Blood lactate values are protocol-, sampling- and timing-dependent; isolated values without context are low-value.

# Decision rules added to Human Performance

1. **Energy systems are concurrent, not switches.** Never prescribe from a simplistic “first ATP-PCr, then glycolytic, then aerobic” timeline.
2. **Lactate != fatigue toxin.** Elevated lactate is a useful metabolic marker in context, not a direct causal diagnosis of fatigue.
3. **Fatigue is multi-causal.** Represent likely contributors with uncertainty; do not infer Pi, H+, K+, glycogen or central fatigue from symptoms alone.
4. **Recovery duration is part of the stimulus.** Shorter recovery changes energetic/metabolic stress and may reduce output quality; longer recovery preserves peak work. Choose based on target.
5. **Threshold method matters.** CP, MLSS, lactate and ventilatory thresholds are related but not automatically interchangeable in an individual.
6. **VO2max is integrated.** It does not identify a single limiting tissue/system.
7. **Acute surrogate != chronic outcome.** Time >=90% VO2max, lactate peak or metabolite response cannot by themselves prove a superior long-term program.
8. **Minimal-friction monitoring.** Routine coaching should use pace/power/HR/RPE/performance before invasive metabolic testing.
9. **Apparent non-response requires error analysis.** Do not personalize aggressively from one noisy pre/post test.
10. **Mechanical cost matters.** Two sessions with similar cardiorespiratory stimulus can have very different tissue costs; modality belongs in the integrated load model.

# Consolidated / provisional / pending map

## CONSOLIDATED
- ATP is immediate energy currency; PCr rapidly buffers ATP turnover.
- Energy pathways overlap continuously.
- Lactate is produced aerobically and functions as fuel/carbon shuttle; it is not simply waste.
- Severe acidosis and Pi can contribute to peripheral fatigue, but fatigue is multi-factorial.
- AMP deamination/purine loss occurs under severe energetic stress.
- Exercise intensity domains are physiologically meaningful.
- VO2max is an integrated systems variable.
- HIIT improves cardiorespiratory fitness across many populations.
- Individual change must be interpreted against measurement error.

## PROVISIONAL / CONTEXT-DEPENDENT
- Exact individual crossover percentages among ATP-PCr/glycolytic/oxidative systems.
- Using CP and MLSS interchangeably without validating protocol.
- Ranking HIIT/SIT/RST as universally superior.
- Selecting one interval protocol from acute time >=90% VO2max.
- Predicting the mechanism of fatigue from RPE, burning, lactate or power loss alone.
- Individual “trainability phenotype” from a single training block.

## PENDING FOR LATER PASSES
- Detailed glycogen compartment biology and fiber-specific depletion/repletion.
- Mitochondrial biogenesis signaling and how much can be operationalized in humans without overinterpreting molecular markers.
- Detailed VO2 slow-component mechanisms and task-specific kinetics.
- Sex-specific and age-specific bioenergetic differences with sufficient practical effect sizes.
- Repeated-sprint recovery models linked to PCr, oxygenation and mechanical output.
- Formal protocol library for CP/MLSS/VT/LT/VO2max testing with reliability and stopping rules.
- Nutrition-specific carbohydrate dosing, creatine, beta-alanine, bicarbonate and nitrate: defer to Sports Nutrition block.

# Research integrity notes

- Prefer human outcome evidence over attractive molecular stories.
- Mechanistic studies explain plausibility; they do not automatically determine the best program.
- Most laboratory mechanistic studies are small and often male-dominant; population transfer should be explicit.
- Threshold nomenclature is inconsistent across the literature. Human Performance should store the actual determination method, not only a label such as “LT2” or “Zone 2”.
